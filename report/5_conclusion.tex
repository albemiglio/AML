\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we proposed and thoroughly evaluated a modular, phase-oriented pipeline for 6D object pose estimation on the LineMod benchmark~\cite{hinterstoisser2012model}. Our analysis shows that, while a ResNet-50 backbone is capable of learning 3D rotations from monocular RGB inputs alone, it suffers catastrophic failure on the translation component due to the inherent ambiguity of projective geometry: the accuracy collapses from 92.9\% (with ground-truth translation) to 1.8\% (with regressed translation), and the pinhole approximation never exceeds 9.4\%. We note that the 92.9\% ceiling under oracle translation is not a tight upper bound on monocular pose estimation, but is itself capped by the residual rotation error of the RGB-only branch ($\sim 12.6^\circ$ at convergence): even with translation fixed to its ground-truth value, this angular error alone displaces object points by $\sim$10\% of the diameter, brushing against the ADD acceptance threshold. Depth supervision is therefore beneficial on \emph{both} axes: in Phase~4 it reduces the rotation error by roughly $2.4\times$ (to $\sim 5.2^\circ$) in addition to driving the translation MSE down by $\sim$2.4 orders of magnitude, which is why the full RGB-D pipeline (98.4\%) outperforms even the RGB-only baseline equipped with the oracle translation. By integrating depth data through a lightweight Global Fusion architecture and enforcing the $\SO$ structural constraint via the 6D continuous rotation representation of Zhou~\etal~\cite{zhou2019continuity}, we recover a 98.4\% ADD accuracy with a Main variant of $\sim$36M parameters, a $\sim$55$\times$ improvement over the RGB-only baseline. The project corroborates the role of proper mathematical representations and well-balanced multi-modal fusion in modern pose estimation pipelines.

\paragraph{Limitations and Future Work}
While the proposed pipeline is accurate and computationally lightweight, we identify four limitations spanning model design and evaluation protocol:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Aspect-ratio distortion.} The current preprocessing uses a square crop with side $\max(w,h)$ followed by direct resize to $224{\times}224$. A square \emph{padding} of the original crop would be mathematically superior, as it preserves the object's aspect ratio and avoids non-isotropic resampling artifacts that perturb the rotation features.
    \item \textbf{The horizontal-flip paradox.} Standard data-augmentation flipping must be disabled in end-to-end 6D pose pipelines: a horizontal mirror of the image corresponds to a non-trivial $\SO$ transformation of the ground-truth pose, and naively flipping the image without re-transforming the label produces an inconsistent supervision signal.
    \item \textbf{Global vs.\ dense fusion.} Our Global Fusion trades pixel-wise spatial alignment for computational efficiency, theoretically reducing resilience against severe occlusions. A natural extension is a sparse-dense hybrid that retains the cheap global pathway while attaching a local per-pixel branch on detected RoIs.
    \item \textbf{Evaluation protocol on sequential benchmarks.} LineMod consists of densely sampled video sequences per object, where consecutive frames differ by $\lesssim$1$^\circ$ of camera rotation. We employ a random 60/20/20 frame-level split, which is the standard practice in the LineMod benchmark. While this split is free of any exact sample overlap (train\,$\cap$\,test\,$=\varnothing$), a quantitative inspection reveals that \emph{84.5\%} of test frames still have a temporal neighbor ($\pm$1 frame, $\approx$0.3$^\circ$ viewpoint) in the training set. We acknowledge that visually similar temporally adjacent frames reflect recognition under near-duplicate-view conditions rather than true cross-domain viewpoint generalization, theoretically raising concerns of dataset overfitting. However, the coherent patterns across classes observed in Table~\ref{tab:perclass} (e.g., the systematic degradation strictly on physically small objects, rather than chaotic failure modes) and the regularizing effect of photometric data augmentation suggest that the model has indeed learned generalizable geometric features and has not simply memorized the training frames. This does not undermine the structural conclusions of the paper -- the relative ordering between phases is preserved because the protocol is applied uniformly -- but it identifies an actionable starting point for follow-ups: a per-object temporal-block split with a buffer gap. We verified that a 70/19/9 layout with a 1\% inter-block gap removes all near-neighbor leakage, turning the test set into a genuine viewpoint-generalization probe.
\end{enumerate}

Addressing the first two points would tighten the upper bound of monocular performance; the third opens a path toward a competitive lightweight alternative to fully dense approaches such as DenseFusion~\cite{wang2019densefusion}; the fourth provides a ready-to-use protocol for assessing viewpoint generalization in future work on LineMod-like sequential benchmarks.
