\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}

\paragraph{Dataset and Metrics}
We evaluate our models on a subset of the LineMod dataset~\cite{hinterstoisser2012model}. Due to data availability in the provided preprocessed source, our subset consists of 13 objects (\textit{Ape, Benchvise, Cam, Can, Cat, Driller, Duck, Eggbox, Glue, Holepuncher, Iron, Lamp, Phone}), excluding the \textit{Bowl} (ID~3) and \textit{Cup} (ID~7) classes which were not present in the local repository. Each object is split 60/20/20 between training, validation and test sets.

Performance is measured using the standard ADD metric (Average Distance of Model Points, Eq.~\ref{eq:add_loss}). A prediction is considered correct if the ADD error is less than 10\% of the object's diameter. For symmetric objects (e.g., \textit{Eggbox}, \textit{Glue}), we adopt the ADD-S variant that computes the closest-point distance.

\paragraph{Training Details}
All pose-estimation models are trained with the Adam optimizer~\cite{kingma2014adam}, initial learning rate $1\!\times\!10^{-4}$, weight decay $1\!\times\!10^{-4}$, batch size~32, for 100 epochs. A \texttt{ReduceLROnPlateau} scheduler halves the learning rate upon validation stagnation. For the Phase~3 baseline we employ a freeze/unfreeze strategy, locking the ResNet-50 backbone for the first 10 epochs to prevent catastrophic forgetting of the ImageNet weights during the initial gradient surge of the randomly initialized regression heads, before unfreezing the backbone for end-to-end fine-tuning. The YOLO11n detector is fine-tuned from ImageNet-pretrained weights for 50 epochs at $640\!\times\!480$ input resolution.

\subsubsection{RGB-only Baseline: the Pinhole Failure (Phase 3)}
The Phase~3 baseline demonstrates that, while a ResNet-50 backbone can effectively learn 3D rotations, it fails dramatically at estimating 3D translation from monocular RGB alone. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:phase3}, the model achieves high accuracy when paired with the ground-truth translation, but performance collapses when the translation is regressed by the network or geometrically inferred via the pinhole approximation (Eq.~\ref{eq:pinhole}).

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Phase~3 baseline accuracy (ADD/ADD-S $<$ 10\% of object diameter).}
\label{tab:phase3}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llc@{}}
\toprule
Configuration & Translation source & Accuracy (\%) \\
\midrule
GT Crop + $\bt_{\text{gt}}$  & Ground truth        & 92.7 \\
YOLO + $\bt_{\text{gt}}$     & Ground truth        & \textbf{92.9} \\
YOLO + $\bt_{\text{pinhole}}$ & Pinhole approx.    & 9.4 \\
YOLO + $\bt_{\text{pred}}$   & Model regression    & \textbf{1.8} \\

\paragraph{Why pinhole fails: a geometric counter-example.}
The collapse to 9.4\% accuracy under the pinhole approximation is not a learning failure but a structural one. The assumption $Z \propto 1/s$ -- where $s = \max(w,h)$ is the bounding-box side -- implicitly treats the object as a sphere whose silhouette size is invariant under rotation. This holds for spherical objects (e.g., the LineMod \textit{Ape}, $\sim$cubic bounding volume), but breaks down for elongated geometries such as the \textit{Driller}: as the object rotates from a frontal to a side view, its 2D bounding-box area can change by a factor of $3\!\times$--$4\!\times$ \emph{without} any change in the object-to-camera distance (Figure~\ref{fig:pinhole_failure}). Any pinhole-based estimator interprets this as a depth swing of equivalent magnitude, producing translation errors of tens of centimeters and dragging ADD accuracy down to single digits. This is precisely the ambiguity that motivates the addition of an explicit depth modality in Phase~4.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figure/pinhole_failure.jpg}
    \caption{Failure case of the pinhole depth approximation on the LineMod \textit{Driller}. The 2D bounding-box area on the side view (left, large bounding box) is multiple times larger than on the frontal view (right, small bounding box), despite the camera being at essentially the same distance. Any estimator that assumes $Z \propto 1/s$ therefore predicts wildly different translations for two physically equivalent configurations, invalidating the monocular distance assumption.}
    \label{fig:pinhole_failure}
\end{figure}

\subsubsection{RGB-D Global Fusion (Phase 4)}
The introduction of depth data and the 6D continuous rotation representation in Phase~4 resolves the depth ambiguity. The Global Fusion architecture (Sec.~\ref{sec:method_fusion}) reaches state-of-the-art performance for a lightweight model on the LineMod subset (Table~\ref{tab:phase4}).

The \emph{Main} variant leverages an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 on a channel-replicated depth map, confirming that transfer learning provides a strong inductive prior even on a non-natural-image modality. Overall, the move from Phase~3 to Phase~4 yields a $\sim$55$\times$ improvement in accuracy ($1.8\% \rightarrow 98.5\%$), validating the central thesis of this work: \emph{multimodal depth fusion combined with a mathematically well-posed rotation parametrization is sufficient to close the gap with computationally expensive dense-fusion baselines}.

\paragraph{Training dynamics}
Figure~\ref{fig:wandb} shows the validation curves of the two phases. The Phase~3 baseline plateaus on rotation (val/deg) around $10$--$15^\circ$ after the backbone unfreezes (step~10), but the loss never drops below $\sim$0.02 because the translation MSE saturates due to the pinhole-related ambiguities discussed above. In Phase~4 (right) the dedicated translation MSE (val/t\_mse) drops by an order of magnitude during the first 30 epochs once depth supervision kicks in, and the ADD loss converges smoothly to $\sim$0.005, mirroring the 98.5\% final accuracy on the test set.

\subsection{Qualitative Analysis}
\label{sec:exp_qualitative}

The Phase~4 Main predictions visualized in Figure~\ref{fig:teaser} illustrate the typical behavior of the model on the test set. On compact, textured objects such as the \textit{Jug} and the \textit{Iron} the predicted box is virtually indistinguishable from the ground truth, with sub-5\,mm ADD error. On the featureless \textit{Ape} (a challenging object due to its lack of discriminative texture) the model achieves $5.77$\,mm ADD, indicating that the depth branch successfully compensates for the limited RGB cues. Failure modes (ADD~$\gtrsim$~10\,mm) are concentrated on geometrically symmetric or partially occluded views of the \textit{Pitcher} and \textit{Camera}: in such cases the rotation head correctly identifies one of the two near-degenerate poses, but the absence of an iterative refinement step (in contrast to DenseFusion~\cite{wang2019densefusion}) prevents the model from breaking the symmetry.

\paragraph{A note on metric normalization.}
A closer inspection of Table~\ref{tab:perclass} reveals that the two underperforming classes, \textit{Ape} (94.8\%) and \textit{Duck} (90.8\%), are also the two smallest objects in the LineMod subset (diameter $\sim$10\,cm, against 25--28\,cm for \textit{Iron}, \textit{Lamp} and \textit{Driller}). Although the ADD$<$10\% acceptance criterion scales \emph{relatively} with the object diameter, this normalization compensates only for object size, not for the coupled effects that depend on it. We identify three structural sources of residual bias: \textbf{(i)} the absolute acceptance tolerance shrinks linearly with diameter ($\sim$10\,mm for \textit{Ape} vs.\ $\sim$28\,mm for \textit{Lamp}), so the same absolute pose-prediction noise crosses the threshold far more often on small objects; \textbf{(ii)} the cropped RGB and depth tensors contain fewer informative pixels per object, lowering the signal-to-noise ratio fed to the backbones; \textbf{(iii)} any pixel-level error in the YOLO bounding box propagates as a larger fraction of the object footprint, perturbing both the RGB texture and the depth crop. The Ape/Duck gap should therefore be read not as a learning failure of the depth branch, but as the residual bias of a metric that is normalized in tolerance but not in input information density. The 100\% scores on the symmetric \textit{Eggbox} and \textit{Glue}, evaluated under the relaxed ADD-S, reinforce this reading by showing that whenever the metric absorbs an additional source of ambiguity, the per-class accuracy saturates.
